Reflections & Lessons Learnt

A candid look at what worked, what was challenging, and how I grew as a tutor across my time supporting Information Systems III and Technical Programming 2.

01 Introduction

Throughout my tutoring journey, I have learned that being a tutor is not only about helping students understand academic content. It is also about learning how to manage responsibilities, communicate effectively, be patient, solve problems, and make a positive contribution to the academic journey of others.

Tutoring Information Systems III and volunteering to assist students with Technical Programming 2 gave me an opportunity to apply my knowledge while developing myself both academically and professionally. Although the experience came with challenges, it was also rewarding because I was able to support students who needed additional assistance.

02 What Worked Well

One of the most rewarding aspects of my tutoring experience was being able to deliver the sessions I had planned and achieve the main objectives I had set for each tutorial. I was able to explain concepts, assist students with questions, conduct activities, and create opportunities for students to practise what they had learned.

Using different approaches during my sessions also worked well. I used PowerPoint presentations to structure my explanations, online quizzes to reinforce concepts, and practical exercises to encourage students to participate. When assisting with Technical Programming 2, I also used Data Structures visualisation tools to demonstrate sorting algorithms. These approaches helped me make technical concepts more practical and easier to understand.

Another positive aspect was seeing students become more comfortable asking questions. Some concepts can be difficult to understand during a formal lecture, and having an additional opportunity to discuss those concepts helped students clarify their understanding.

Tutoring also reinforced my own academic knowledge. When I had to explain a concept to someone else, I had to make sure that I understood it properly myself. In this way, tutoring became a learning experience for me as well.

03 Challenges I Experienced

One of my biggest challenges was balancing my tutoring responsibilities with my own academic workload. I had my own assignments, tests, projects, and other academic responsibilities that required my attention. At the same time, I had to prepare for and conduct tutorial sessions.

The workload became even greater when I agreed to assist students in Technical Programming 2 after they expressed that they needed additional help, particularly with Data Structures. I wanted to assist them because I had knowledge of the subject and knew that I could contribute. However, taking on this additional responsibility meant that I had to manage my academic work and tutoring commitments at the same time.

I will not say that it was always easy. There were times when I had a lot of work to complete and still had to prepare for tutoring. This experience taught me that helping others is rewarding, but it also requires proper planning and responsible management of my time.

Another challenge was that students did not always have the same level of understanding. Some students understood a concept quickly, while others required additional explanations and examples. This meant that I sometimes had to change my approach during a session to make sure that students who were struggling were not left behind.

04 How I Handled the Challenges

The main strategy I used to manage these challenges was time management. I realised that when you have multiple responsibilities, you cannot simply focus on one task and hope that everything else will work out. You need to plan your time and understand what needs to be completed first.

I started paying closer attention to deadlines and planning my academic work around my tutoring responsibilities. I allocated time for studying, completing assignments, preparing tutorials, conducting sessions, and also making time for my personal and social life.

I also learned to prioritise tasks according to their urgency and importance. When I knew that I had a tutorial coming up, I prepared in advance rather than waiting until the last minute. This reduced pressure and allowed me to focus properly on the students during the session.

Most importantly, I reminded myself why I agreed to become a tutor in the first place. I wanted to use the knowledge and skills I had gained to help other students. Even when I was busy, I reminded myself that if I had the ability to help someone understand something that I already knew, then I should try to help where I could. This mindset helped me remain committed to my responsibilities.

At the same time, I learned that helping others does not mean neglecting my own responsibilities. I had to find a balance between supporting students and making sure that my own academic progress remained a priority.

05 What I Learned

One of the biggest lessons I learned is that knowledge becomes more valuable when you are able to share it with others. Understanding something yourself is different from explaining it to someone who is struggling with the same concept. Tutoring taught me to communicate knowledge in a way that other people can understand.

I also learned the importance of patience. Students learn at different speeds, and a tutor needs to understand that asking the same question more than once does not mean that a student is not capable. Sometimes a student simply needs the concept explained differently.

Another important lesson was that time management is essential. Balancing my own studies, tutoring, personal responsibilities, and social life taught me how important it is to plan ahead and use my time effectively.

I also developed greater confidence in speaking to groups and explaining technical concepts. Before tutoring, I was more focused on understanding concepts for my own academic work. Through tutoring, I had to think about how another person would understand those concepts. This improved my communication and presentation skills.

The experience also strengthened my leadership skills. I learned that leadership is not always about being the person in charge. Sometimes leadership means being available to help someone, sharing knowledge, listening to their problems, and guiding them towards finding their own solutions.

06 What I Would Improve

Although I am satisfied with my tutoring experience, there are several areas I would improve in future tutoring sessions.

01

Time allocation during tutorials

I would make sure that each activity has a clearly defined amount of time so that I can cover the important learning objectives without rushing at the end of the session.

02

Greater use of student feedback

Although I could identify some difficulties through questions and participation, collecting structured feedback would help me understand what students found useful and what could be improved.

03

Wider range of activities

I would prepare basic exercises for students who need more practice and more challenging problems for students who are already comfortable with the topic.

04

Balancing additional tutoring requests

I am willing to help students whenever I can, but I have learned that I also need to consider my available time and academic responsibilities before taking on additional commitments.

07 My Growth Throughout the Tutoring Period

Looking back at the beginning of my tutoring journey, I can see growth in both my confidence and my ability to work with students.

I have become more comfortable presenting information, answering questions, managing tutorial sessions, and adapting my explanations when students do not immediately understand a concept. I have also become more aware of the importance of preparation and planning.

Tutoring has strengthened skills that are directly relevant to my future career as an Applications Developer. Communication, teamwork, problem-solving, time management, leadership, adaptability, and the ability to understand users' needs are all important in software development.

Most importantly, this experience has taught me that being successful is not only about achieving my own goals. It is also about being able to contribute to the success of others. Helping students understand concepts that they initially found difficult gave me a sense of purpose and reminded me of the value of sharing knowledge.

08 Overall Reflection

Overall, my tutoring experience has been challenging, rewarding, and valuable. There were moments when balancing my academic responsibilities with tutoring was difficult, especially after taking on additional support for Technical Programming 2. However, the experience taught me how to manage my time, remain committed to my responsibilities, and continue helping others even when things became demanding.

I leave this tutoring experience with greater confidence, stronger communication and leadership skills, and a better understanding of what it means to support other students. I believe these lessons will continue to benefit me throughout my academic journey and in my future career as an Applications Developer.
