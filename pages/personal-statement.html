Personal Statement

Who I am, why I tutor, and what I hope to achieve a reflective account of my academic journey and my role as a tutor in the Faculty of Engineering, Built Environment and Information Technology.

01 Introduction

My name is Olwabo Salelo, and I am currently pursuing an Advanced Diploma in ICT in Applications Development in the Faculty of Engineering, Built Environment and Information Technology (FEBIT), within the Department of Application Development and Business. I previously completed my Diploma in ICT in Applications Development with Cum Laude, achieving an overall average of 82%. My academic journey has strengthened my passion for technology, application development, problem-solving, and helping others develop their understanding of ICT.

As part of my tutoring responsibilities, I currently provide academic support to third-year students in Information Systems 3. I have also volunteered to assist students with Technical Programming 2. Through these experiences, I have had the opportunity to share the knowledge and skills I have gained during my studies while supporting students who may experience challenges with technical and theoretical concepts. My long-term goal is to become an Applications Developer, where I can use my technical knowledge and creativity to develop innovative software solutions that solve real-world problems.

02 Why I Became a Tutor

I decided to become a tutor because I understand the value of academic support and the difference that peer guidance can make in a student's learning journey. Having progressed through the ICT in Applications Development programme myself, I understand that some concepts can be challenging, particularly when students are introduced to complex programming, information systems, and problem-solving concepts.

Tutoring gives me an opportunity to share my knowledge and assist other students in overcoming these challenges. I believe that learning should not only involve receiving information from a lecturer or tutor but should also involve discussion, collaboration, questioning, and learning from one another.

The tutoring programme also provides an opportunity for my own personal and professional development. Through tutoring, I am able to strengthen important skills such as communication, leadership, teamwork, time management, problem-solving, and public speaking. These skills are valuable not only in an academic environment but also in my future career as an Applications Developer.

Another important reason I enjoy tutoring is that teaching others strengthens my own understanding. When I explain a concept to another student, I am required to understand it well enough to explain it clearly and from different perspectives. This encourages continuous learning and allows me to identify areas where I can improve my own knowledge.

03 My Approach to Tutoring

My approach to tutoring is centred around creating a supportive, inclusive, and student-focused learning environment. I see myself not only as someone who provides answers, but as a facilitator who guides students towards understanding concepts and developing their own problem-solving abilities.

I believe that students learn differently, and therefore I try to use different approaches depending on the needs of the students. Where possible, I use practical examples, discussions, demonstrations, questions, and problem-solving activities to make concepts easier to understand and relate to real-world situations.

I also encourage students to actively participate during tutorials rather than simply listening. I want students to feel comfortable asking questions, sharing their ideas, and making mistakes because mistakes are an important part of the learning process.

As an ICT student and tutor, I also recognise the importance of technology in education. I aim to make appropriate use of digital tools and resources to support learning and make tutorials more interactive and accessible.

Inclusive

Every student deserves a space where they feel seen and heard.

Practical

Concepts become clear when tied to real code and real problems.

Conversational

Discussion, questioning and dialogue drive deeper understanding.

04 My Goals as a Tutor

My goals as a tutor are focused on improving student understanding, participation, confidence, and academic performance.

01

Personalised Support

I aim to provide guidance based on the different needs and levels of understanding of the students I support. I want to identify areas where students are struggling and provide additional explanations or practical examples where necessary.

02

Strategic Preparation

I commit to preparing thoroughly before each tutorial session. I aim to structure my sessions with a clear introduction, learning objectives, main content or activities, opportunities for questions and discussion, and a conclusion that reinforces the key concepts.

03

Active Engagement

I aim to use interactive activities such as discussions, quizzes, practical exercises, problem-solving activities, and group work to encourage students to participate actively in tutorials.

04

Practical Learning

I want to connect academic concepts to real-world situations, particularly those related to ICT and application development. This can help students understand not only what they are learning but also why the knowledge is useful.

05

Building Student Confidence

I want to create an environment where students feel comfortable asking questions and expressing difficulties without feeling judged. My goal is to help students become more confident in their academic abilities.

06

Continuous Improvement

I aim to use feedback from students, lecturers, and fellow tutors to continuously improve my tutoring approach. I believe that being a good tutor requires me to remain an active learner as well.

Ultimately, my goal is to make a meaningful contribution to the academic development of the students I tutor. I want my tutorials to be more than just sessions where content is explained; I want them to be spaces where students can learn, participate, collaborate, ask questions, solve problems, and build confidence.

At the same time, I see tutoring as an important part of my own development. The experience allows me to grow as a communicator, leader, and future ICT professional while giving me the opportunity to contribute positively to the academic community.
